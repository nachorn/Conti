# How to Play — Conti (Continental)

## Overview

**Conti** is a seven-round rummy game. Each round has a contract made from trios and straights. Players draw, optionally play or add cards, and then discard. After Round 7, the player with the **lowest total score** wins.

## Setup

- **Players:** 2–10.
- **Decks:** The host chooses 2 or 3 standard 52-card decks. Each deck also contains **3 Jokers**, for 55 cards per deck.
- **Large games:** With more than 5 players, choosing 2 decks displays a warning because 2 decks may not provide enough cards for later rounds. The host may still continue or choose 3 decks.
- **Cards per round:** Round 1 deals 7 cards to each player, Round 2 deals 8, and so on through 13 cards in Round 7.

## Card Values and Wilds

- **Wild cards:** Only **Jokers** are wild. Twos always play naturally as rank 2.
- **Cards left in hand:**
  - Joker: **50 points**
  - Ace: **20 points**
  - King, Queen, or Jack: **10 points**
  - Number cards 2–10: **face value**

## Melds

Two types of melds are used:

1. **Trio** — 3 or more cards of the same rank, such as 7♥ 7♦ 7♣. Jokers may substitute, but natural cards must outnumber Jokers.
2. **Straight (escala)** — 4 or more consecutive cards of the same suit, such as 5♥ 6♥ 7♥ 8♥. An Ace may be low in A-2-3-4 or high in J-Q-K-A, but a straight cannot wrap through the Ace. Natural cards must outnumber Jokers, and two Joker-filled positions cannot be consecutive.

## Round Contracts

| Round | Cards dealt | Contract |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 7 | 2 trios, each at least 3 cards |
| 2 | 8 | 1 trio of at least 3 + 1 straight of at least 4 |
| 3 | 9 | 2 straights, each at least 4 cards |
| 4 | 10 | 3 trios, each at least 3 cards |
| 5 | 11 | 2 trios of at least 3 + 1 straight of at least 4 |
| 6 | 12 | 2 straights of at least 4 + 1 trio of at least 3 |
| 7 | 13 | 3 straights, each at least 4 cards |

You must **go down** by playing the complete contract at once. Your initial play may contain only the contract's required number and types of standalone melds. A required meld may be longer than its minimum, but you cannot include an extra meld—for example, Round 1 allows exactly two trios, not a third trio in the same play. Partial contracts cannot be saved across turns. Before the first contract can be played, every player must have completed at least one normal turn.

Each player may play their round contract only once. After going down, you cannot create another standalone trio or straight. You may only:

- Add cards to one of your existing melds.
- Add cards to another player's melds.
- Replace a Joker in a straight according to the rules below.
- Discard.

## Turn Flow

1. **Draw** one card from the stock or take the top discard when available.
2. **Play**, if desired: play your complete contract, add cards after going down, or perform a legal Joker replacement.
3. **Discard** one card to end the turn, unless playing or adding cards has already emptied your hand and ended the round.

Before going down, you cannot discard a card that can legally be added to a meld already on the table.

## Taking or Passing a Discard

After a player discards, the next player in turn order has priority:

- If the priority player takes the discard, it is their normal draw and they continue their turn.
- If they pass, each following player gets an opportunity in turn order.
- A later player who takes it receives both the discard and **one penalty card from the stock**. Buying the discard does not give that player the turn; the original priority player still plays next and draws normally.
- If everyone passes, the priority player begins their turn and may still draw from the stock or take the top discard.

## Replacing Jokers

A Joker may be replaced **only in a straight**, never in a trio. There are two cases:

### Before you have gone down

You may replace a Joker in another player's straight with the exact natural card it represents only if the reclaimed Joker lets you play your **entire round contract during that same turn**. The Joker must be used in that contract. The replacement is rejected if the complete contract cannot also be played; you cannot take the Joker into your hand for later.

### After you have gone down

You may replace a Joker in your own or another player's straight with the exact natural card it represents. The Joker must remain in that same straight and move to a legal exposed end; it never enters your hand and cannot be moved to a different meld.

For example, in `2♦ 3♦ Joker 5♦`, a `4♦` replaces the Joker. The Joker may then extend that same straight as `A♦` on the low end or `6♦` on the high end. If the Joker cannot be legally placed at either exposed end, the replacement is not allowed.

## Going Out and Scoring

A player goes out as soon as they have no cards left. This can happen by:

- Playing every card as part of the initial contract.
- Playing the contract and discarding the one remaining card during that same turn.
- Playing the contract and adding the final remaining card to another legal meld during that same turn.
- Emptying the hand on a later turn by adding or discarding the final card.

The round winner receives:

- **−10 × the round number** when they go out on the same turn that they play their contract, including when one final card is discarded or added. This is −10 in Round 1 through −70 in Round 7.
- **−10 points** when they go out on a later turn after previously playing their contract.

Every other player adds the value of the cards remaining in their hand to their total score. Scores can therefore be negative, and the lowest cumulative score wins.

After Round 7 ends, the game displays the final scoreboard and winner.

## Optional Lobby Settings

- **Turn timer:** Optional limit for each turn; zero means no limit.
- **Take/pass delay:** Optional delay before the discard decision becomes available.
