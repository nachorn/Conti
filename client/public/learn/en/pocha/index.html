POCHA GUIDE

How to play Pocha

Before playing your cards, predict exactly how many tricks you will win. Accuracy matters more than winning lots of tricks: the highest final score wins.

  • 2–10 players
  • Spanish deck
  • Normal or auction
  • Highest score wins

Set up a game

Create a Pocha room on the home page, share its invitation link or code and wait for your friends to connect. The host chooses the deck, mode and round schedule inside the room. Everyone sees those settings before play begins.

The four suits are oros (coins), copas (cups), espadas (swords) and bastos (clubs). Choose one of four decks:

  • 48 cards: Ace through King, including Eights and Nines.
  • 40 cards: no Eights or Nines.
  • 36 cards: Fours are also removed.
  • 32 cards: Twos are also removed.

From strongest to weakest, the trick order is Ace, Three, King, Knight, Jack, Nine, Eight, Seven, Six, Five, Four, Two, using only ranks present in the chosen deck. The printed number is not a simple strength scale: a Three beats a King of the same suit.

The round schedule

A game begins with a block of one-card rounds, rises to the chosen maximum and falls again. The host chooses how many one-card rounds appear at each end and how often the maximum repeats. For example, a three-card maximum with one repetition per block produces 1, 2, 3, 2, 1. A one-card-only game has just one block, without a repeated ending.

The maximum cannot exceed the cards available when dealing equally to everyone. Check the full schedule preview in the room before starting. The first opening position is chosen randomly and rotates in subsequent rounds.

Make your prediction

Every player receives the same number of cards each round. In normal mode, a turned-up card sets the trump suit. If no cards remain after dealing, the last dealt card sets trump instead. Players then predict how many tricks they will win, from zero up to the number of cards in their hand.

The final player to predict cannot choose a number that makes all predictions add up exactly to the available tricks. At least one prediction must therefore be wrong.

The prediction you cannot make

In a four-card round, Ana predicts one trick and Bruno predicts two. That leaves 4 − 1 − 2 = 1. If Carla predicts last, she cannot choose one. Zero, two, three and four are allowed.

Your prediction is fixed for that round. Winning more tricks than predicted is still a miss, so sometimes you will want to give away a trick you could win.

Play a trick

A trick contains one card from each player. The opening card sets the led suit, and everyone plays in order. The highest trump wins; if nobody plays trump, the highest card of the led suit wins. The winner leads the next trick.

Which cards must you play?

  1. Follow the led suit if you have it. If one of those cards can beat the current winning card, you must play a card that does. Otherwise, choose any card of the led suit.
  2. Without the led suit, play a winning trump if you can. This also requires beating a trump already played when possible.
  3. Without the led suit or a trump that can win, choose any card. You are not forced to waste a lower trump that cannot win the trick.

Conti marks the cards you can play. Select a card, then confirm it. After a trick is complete, its winner is shown briefly before play resumes.

A small trump can win

Suppose coins are trump. Ana leads Six of Cups, and Bruno plays Three of Cups. Carla has no cups and holds Two of Coins.

Ana · lead

3cups

Bruno · higher

2coins

Carla · trump

Carla must play a winning trump if she has one. The Two of Coins wins, even though a Three is stronger within its own suit, because coins are trump. Carla leads the next trick.

If Carla held a cup, she would have to follow suit instead of trumping. If an Ace of Coins had already been played and she had no cups, her Two of Coins could not win; she could then discard any card.

Score the round

Score each player after all tricks in the round. An exact prediction earns 5 points plus 2 per trick won. A miss loses 2 points for every trick of difference, whether you won too many or too few.

What changes in auction Pocha?

Rounds at the chosen maximum hand size use an auction, even if cards remain undealt. All other rounds use the normal mode.

  1. Each player has one opportunity. The first opens with a number; later players must raise it or pass.
  2. The highest offer becomes its winner’s fixed trick prediction.
  3. The auction winner chooses trump and leads the first trick.
  4. Everyone else predicts in the round’s original order, skipping the winner because their prediction is already set.

The next round keeps the game’s original rotation. Auctions do not change the scoring or the requirements to follow suit and beat the winning card.

A first-game tip

Before predicting, consider trump and the number of players. An Ace is strong, but a card of a different suit cannot beat a trump. Keep an eye on your tricks won and your prediction so you know when to win and when to yield.
