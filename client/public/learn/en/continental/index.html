CONTINENTAL GUIDE

How to play Continental Rummy

Build trios and straights, play each round’s contract and empty your hand. The player with the lowest total after seven rounds wins Continental.

  • 2–10 players
  • 2 or 3 decks
  • 7 rounds
  • Lowest score wins

Set up the table

On the home page, enter your name under “Create Continental”, choose two or three decks and create the room. Share its invitation link or four-digit room code with your friends. Once at least two players are connected, the host can start.

Each French-suited deck has 52 regular cards and three Jokers. For more than five players, three decks are advisable because later rounds deal many cards. The room also offers an optional turn limit and a delay before taking or passing a discard. A turn limit of zero means no limit.

This guide describes the rules used by Conti. Families often use different variants, so check the discard-buying and Joker rules together before starting.

The seven contracts

A trio is at least three cards of the same rank. A straight is at least four consecutive cards of one suit. An Ace can be low in A–2–3–4 or high in J–Q–K–A, but a straight cannot wrap from King through Ace to Two.

To go down, play the complete contract in one action. Its melds may be longer than the minimum, but you cannot add an extra standalone trio or straight. Round 1, for example, requires exactly two trios. Before anyone can go down, every player must have completed at least one normal turn.

Once down, you cannot create more standalone melds. You may extend your own or another player’s melds as long as they remain valid.

Your turn: draw, play and discard

  1. Draw a card. Choose the stock or the top discard when available. Sorting your hand by rank or suit can help you see which cards you need.
  2. Play if you can and want to. Lay down the full contract, or add cards to the table if you are already down.
  3. Discard one card. Select it and use the discard action. If playing cards empties your hand, the round ends immediately without a discard.

Taking, passing and buying a discard

After a discard, the next player has priority. Taking it counts as that player’s normal draw. If they pass, other players receive the choice in turn order. A later player who takes it buys it: they receive the discard plus one penalty card from the stock. Buying does not give them the turn; the original priority player still plays next and draws normally.

If everyone passes, the priority player starts their turn and may still choose the stock or top discard. Before going down, you cannot discard a card that can legally be added to a meld already on the table.

WORKED TURN

Going out in the first round

Suppose you have these eight cards after drawing, and every player has already had a turn:

7♥7♦7♣9♥9♦9♣9♠K♠

Play a trio of Sevens and a four-card trio of Nines. A trio can contain more than three cards, so both melds are valid. Discard the remaining King to go out on the same turn you went down and score −10 points. Everyone else adds the value of the cards left in their hand.

This play would not satisfy Round 2, which requires one trio and one straight rather than two trios. Always check the current contract displayed at the table.

Using Jokers

Only Jokers are wild; Twos always keep their natural rank. Natural cards must outnumber Jokers in every trio or straight. A straight also cannot have two consecutive positions filled by Jokers.

A valid example is 5♥, 6♥, Joker, 8♥, where the Joker represents 7♥. Two natural cards and two Jokers are not enough because naturals must be the majority.

Replacing a Joker in a straight

Use the exact natural card it represents. Jokers in trios cannot be reclaimed.

  • Before going down: you may replace one in another player’s straight only if the reclaimed Joker lets you play your complete contract that same turn, using the Joker in it. You cannot keep it for later.
  • After going down: the Joker stays in that same straight and moves to a legal exposed end. It does not enter your hand or move to another meld.

In 2♦, 3♦, Joker, 5♦, a 4♦ replaces the Joker. For a player already down, that Joker can then extend the straight as 6♦, or as A♦ if that end is legal. If neither end works, the replacement is not allowed.

Going out and scoring

The round ends when someone has no cards left, whether by playing, adding or discarding. Going out on the same turn you played your contract earns −10 × the round number, from −10 in Round 1 to −70 in Round 7. If you went down on an earlier turn, going out earns −10.

For example, an unplayed Ace, Queen and Five score 20 + 10 + 5 = 35 points. Add all seven rounds; the lowest total wins, even if it is negative.

For your first game

Check the contract before deciding which cards to keep. Weigh a discard purchase carefully: a useful card also brings a penalty card into your hand. Once you are down, inspect the whole table, because you may add cards to anyone’s melds.
